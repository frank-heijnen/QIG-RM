1. Why we changed it
--------------------
The previous FX VaR calculation used the parametric formula

    VaR_1M^95  =  z_95  *  (USD_equity_in_EUR / NAV_EUR)  *  NAV_EUR  *  sigma_FX * sqrt(1/12)

with sigma_FX hard-coded at 5% and only USD/EUR in scope. Two problems:

  (a) It treats every USD-denominated stock as if it were pure USD cash,
      i.e. 100% exposed to EUR/USD. Most US large-caps we hold (GOOG,
      LLY, UNH, NOW, NEM, ...) are multinationals with substantial foreign
      revenues. When the dollar weakens, their foreign earnings translate
      into more USD and the share price tends to rise -- a natural hedge
      that the old formula ignored entirely.

  (b) sigma_FX was a fixed number rather than something measured from recent
      data.

  (c) Only USD was in scope. The actual portfolio also carries CHF cash and
      a DKK-listed equity + DKK cash, which the old number did not touch.

Together these shortcuts made the reported FX VaR materially larger than
the portfolio's true economic FX risk.


2. What the new calculation does
--------------------------------
Step 1 -- Classify holdings by currency of listing
    Positions are read from the broker statement and tagged by listing
    currency (USD / EUR / CHF / DKK). Cash is tagged likewise. Anything in
    EUR contributes zero FX risk by definition.

Step 2 -- Empirical FX volatilities and covariance
    For every non-EUR currency we download daily EUR/ccy prices over the
    last ~2 years, compute daily log-returns and annualize with sqrt(252).
    We also keep the full FX covariance matrix for the portfolio-level VaR.

Step 3 -- FX beta per stock
    For each non-EUR stock i we regress its local-currency daily log-return
    on the corresponding FX log-return:

        r_i,ccy = alpha_i + beta_i * r_FX + epsilon_i.

    beta_i measures how sensitive stock i's local-currency price is to a
    move in its own currency. Typical US multinationals have beta_i < 0
    (stock rises when USD weakens).

Step 4 -- Effective FX factor loading
    Translating stock i to EUR gives

        r_i,EUR = r_i,ccy + r_FX
                = alpha_i + (1 + beta_i) * r_FX + epsilon_i,

    so the stock's effective loading on the FX factor is (1 + beta_i), NOT
    1. A stock with beta_i = -0.4 only contributes 60% of its EUR market
    value to our FX exposure.

    Cash in a foreign currency has no stock-level natural hedge, so its
    factor loading stays at 1.

Step 5 -- Per-currency effective notional
    For each non-EUR currency c:

        N_eff_c = sum over stocks in c of (1 + beta_i) * V_i   +   cash_c.

Step 6 -- Parametric VaR
    Per currency:

        VaR_1M^95,c = z_95 * |N_eff_c| * sigma_c * sqrt(1/12).

    Portfolio-level, using the full annualized FX covariance matrix Sigma:

        sigma^2_port,1M = n^T Sigma n * (1/12),
        VaR_1M^95       = z_95 * sigma_port,1M,

    where n is the vector of effective notionals across non-EUR currencies.
    The covariance form matters because EUR/USD and EUR/CHF are
    correlated; ignoring this would overstate (or understate) the
    portfolio-level number.


3. Notes on the current portfolio
---------------------------------
  - USD is the dominant FX exposure: 11 equities plus USD cash.
  - CHF is cash-only; loading = 1 (pure translation risk, no hedge).
  - DKK is effectively pegged to EUR (ERM II band +/- 2.25%, defended by
    Danmarks Nationalbank). Empirical EUR/DKK vol is typically below 1%,
    so PNDORA and DKK cash contribute negligible FX risk. The notebook
    computes the number anyway rather than hard-coding a "zero".


4. Sanity check
---------------
If every beta_i were 0 and all non-EUR holdings were cash, (1 + beta_i)
collapses to 1, N_eff equals the gross notional, and the new formula
reduces to the old one (up to the covariance structure). The gap between
the new and old numbers is therefore a clean measure of the natural-hedge
benefit that the old formula was ignoring.


5. Operational notes for the monthly run
----------------------------------------
  - Update `positions` and `cash_by_ccy` in section 1 of the notebook from
    the broker statement each month. Holdings screenshots live in
    `holdings/` (gitignored so we don't leak private broker data).
  - The historical window for sigmas / betas is controlled by
    `hist_start` / `hist_end`. Two years of daily data is a reasonable
    default; shorten it if there has been a structural break in FX regime.
  - The notebook prints both a console summary and a LaTeX snippet that
    can be pasted straight into the monthly risk report.
