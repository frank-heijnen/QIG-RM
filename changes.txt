1. Why we changed it
--------------------
The previous FX VaR calculation used the parametric formula

    VaR_1M^95  =  z_95  *  (USD_equity_in_EUR / NAV_EUR)  *  NAV_EUR  *  sigma_FX * sqrt(1/12)

with sigma_FX hard-coded at 5% and only USD/EUR in scope. Two problems:

  (a) It treats every USD-denominated stock as if it were pure USD cash,
      i.e. 100% exposed to EUR/USD. Most US large-caps we hold (GOOG,
      LLY, UNH, NOW, NEM, ...) are multinationals with substantial foreign
      revenues. When the dollar weakens, their foreign earnings translate
      into more USD and the share price tends to rise -- a natural hedge
      that the old formula ignored entirely.

  (b) sigma_FX was a fixed number rather than something measured from recent
      data.

  (c) Only USD was in scope. The actual portfolio also carries CHF cash and
      a DKK-listed equity + DKK cash, which the old number did not touch.

Together these shortcuts made the reported FX VaR materially larger than
the portfolio's true economic FX risk.


2. What the new calculation does
--------------------------------
Step 1 -- Classify holdings by currency of listing
    Positions are read from the broker statement and tagged by listing
    currency (USD / EUR / CHF / DKK). Cash is tagged likewise. Anything in
    EUR contributes zero FX risk by definition.

Step 2 -- Empirical FX volatilities and covariance
    For every non-EUR currency we download daily EUR/ccy prices over the
    last ~2 years, compute daily log-returns and annualize with sqrt(252).
    We also keep the full FX covariance matrix for the portfolio-level VaR.

Step 3 -- FX beta per stock
    For each non-EUR stock i we regress its local-currency daily log-return
    on the corresponding FX log-return:

        r_i,ccy = alpha_i + beta_i * r_FX + epsilon_i.

    beta_i measures how sensitive stock i's local-currency price is to a
    move in its own currency. Typical US multinationals have beta_i < 0
    (stock rises when USD weakens).

Step 4 -- Effective FX factor loading
    Translating stock i to EUR gives

        r_i,EUR = r_i,ccy + r_FX
                = alpha_i + (1 + beta_i) * r_FX + epsilon_i,

    so the stock's effective loading on the FX factor is (1 + beta_i), NOT
    1. A stock with beta_i = -0.4 only contributes 60% of its EUR market
    value to our FX exposure.

    Cash in a foreign currency has no stock-level natural hedge, so its
    factor loading stays at 1.

Step 5 -- Per-currency effective notional
    For each non-EUR currency c:

        N_eff_c = sum over stocks in c of (1 + beta_i) * V_i   +   cash_c.

Step 6 -- Parametric VaR
    Per currency:

        VaR_1M^95,c = z_95 * |N_eff_c| * sigma_c * sqrt(1/12).

    Portfolio-level, using the full annualized FX covariance matrix Sigma:

        sigma^2_port,1M = n^T Sigma n * (1/12),
        VaR_1M^95       = z_95 * sigma_port,1M,

    where n is the vector of effective notionals across non-EUR currencies.
    The covariance form matters because EUR/USD and EUR/CHF are
    correlated; ignoring this would overstate (or understate) the
    portfolio-level number.


3. Notes on the current portfolio
---------------------------------
  - USD is the dominant FX exposure: 11 equities plus USD cash.
  - CHF is cash-only; loading = 1 (pure translation risk, no hedge).
  - DKK is effectively pegged to EUR (ERM II band +/- 2.25%, defended by
    Danmarks Nationalbank). Empirical EUR/DKK vol is typically below 1%,
    so PNDORA and DKK cash contribute negligible FX risk. The notebook
    computes the number anyway rather than hard-coding a "zero".


4. Sanity check
---------------
If every beta_i were 0 and all non-EUR holdings were cash, (1 + beta_i)
collapses to 1, N_eff equals the gross notional, and the new formula
reduces to the old one (up to the covariance structure). The gap between
the new and old numbers is therefore a clean measure of the natural-hedge
benefit that the old formula was ignoring.


5. Operational notes for the monthly run
----------------------------------------
  - Update `positions` and `cash_by_ccy` in section 1 of the notebook from
    the broker statement each month. Holdings screenshots live in
    `holdings/` (gitignored so we don't leak private broker data).
  - The historical window for sigmas / betas is controlled by
    `hist_start` / `hist_end`. Two years of daily data is a reasonable
    default; shorten it if there has been a structural break in FX regime.
  - The notebook prints both a console summary and a LaTeX snippet that
    can be pasted straight into the monthly risk report.

1. Why this exists
------------------
Model risk is a core risk-management responsibility, and so far none of
our internal risk models (the parametric FX VaR, the GBM and GARCH(1,1)
Monte-Carlo engines in models/Portfolio.py, the historical-simulation VaR
used elsewhere) are validated in any systematic way. The existing
`backtesting/` folder is for *strategy* research -- it answers "does this
signal make money" and is not the right place to test whether our risk
numbers are calibrated.

The framework introduced here is modelled on two pieces of regulatory
practice:

  (a) The Basel VaR backtesting cycle (traffic-light test on 1-day 99%
      VaR over a rolling 250-day window), and the two standard
      statistical tests that go with it -- Kupiec's proportion-of-failures
      (unconditional coverage) and Christoffersen's independence /
      conditional-coverage tests.

  (b) PLAT (the P&L Attribution Test from FRTB), which compares
      Hypothetical P&L (HPL -- the portfolio revalued under static
      positions) with Risk-Theoretical P&L (RTPL -- the portfolio
      revalued using only the risk factors the model sees). PLAT catches
      *missing* risk factors, which coverage tests alone cannot.

v1 implements (a). (b) is deferred -- see section 5.

                                       TestResult dataclasses.

A new folder rather than extending `backtesting/` because the two do
genuinely different jobs; keeping them separated avoids future confusion
about which backtest someone ran.


3. The model protocol
---------------------
Every VaR model plugs in by implementing two methods:

    class VaRModel(Protocol):
        name: str
        def calibrate(self, history: pd.DataFrame) -> None: ...
        def forecast(self, positions: pd.Series | None,
                     horizon: int = 1, alpha: float = 0.99) -> VaRForecast: ...

Splitting calibration from forecasting matters because GARCH-style
re-estimation is expensive but positions change every day. The runner
calls `calibrate` on a user-chosen cadence (daily / weekly / monthly /
every N days) and `forecast` every day.

`VaRForecast` carries the number itself and an optional ES, so the same
harness can later run ES backtests (Acerbi-Sz\'ekely) without signature
changes. The no-lookahead guarantee is enforced by the runner: models
are only ever handed `history.loc[history.index < t]`.


4. The runner and the diagnostic tests
--------------------------------------
run_backtest(model, history, positions, hpl, window, alpha, horizon,
             refit_every) produces a DataFrame indexed by date with
columns (var, es, hpl, breach). Breach = hpl < -var.

Given that DataFrame, the diagnostics module offers:

  - kupiec_pof(breaches, alpha)                -- unconditional coverage
        LR = -2 * log(L0 / L1), chi2(1). Tests whether the realised
        breach rate equals (1 - alpha).

  - christoffersen_independence(breaches)      -- serial independence
        Likelihood-ratio on the first-order Markov transition counts.
        Catches "clustered breaches" that Kupiec misses.

  - christoffersen_cc(breaches, alpha)         -- joint conditional
        coverage test, LR_cc = LR_uc + LR_ind, chi2(2).

  - rolling_traffic_light(breaches, window=250)
        For each day, the trailing-250 breach count, zone (green <=4,
        yellow 5-9, red >=10) and the Basel VaR multiplier
        (3.0 in green; 3.0 + {0.40, 0.50, 0.65, 0.75, 0.85} across
        yellow; 4.0 in red).

These are the "first four numbers" you would put in any presentation
about VaR model validity.


5. What v1 does NOT contain (deliberate)
----------------------------------------
  - PLAT (HPL vs RTPL). Needs a risk-factor decomposition none of our
    models currently expose. The slot is there -- the protocol can be
    extended to return the factor set used -- but waiting until at
    least one model has a clean factor representation.

  - Multi-horizon VaR. The signature accepts `horizon` but only 1-day is
    implemented. The GARCH adapter will need horizon > 1 eventually.

  - The parametric FX adapter. The logic currently lives inside
    macro/Exchange_rate_risk.ipynb and is not importable from a .py
    module. Next step is to extract it into macro/fx_risk.py; after
    that, adapters/parametric_fx.py can wrap it in two screens of code.

  - Historical position snapshots. None exist in any archive we have
    access to, so the harness operates under a constant-book assumption
    (see the pivot section at the bottom of this file). `compute_hpl_eur`
    and the new `constant_book_frames` helper together convert today's
    broker-statement book into the daily quantity and EUR-value frames
    the runner needs.


6. First concrete adapter -- HistoricalSimulationVaR
----------------------------------------------------
A deliberately simple model: VaR at level alpha is the (1-alpha)
empirical quantile over a trailing window of portfolio daily P&L,
ES is the mean of the losses beyond that quantile. It ignores positions
and works at portfolio level. Purpose: smoke-test the harness end-to-end
without depending on anything else.


7. Smoke-test run
-----------------
A 780-day synthetic EUR P&L series (normal, sigma 10k, two injected
shocks of -80k and -60k) was run through the harness with a 250-day
calibration window, monthly refit, 99% VaR, and a 519-day test window.

Output highlights:

  Total days: 519
  Breaches:   9       (1.73%, expected 1.00%)

  Kupiec POF            LR = 2.32,  p = 0.128   -> do not reject
  Christoffersen ind    LR = 0.32,  p = 0.573   -> do not reject
  Christoffersen CC     LR = 2.64,  p = 0.268   -> do not reject
  Rolling traffic light end-of-period: green zone, multiplier 3.0

The two injected shocks produce two breach clusters within 250 days of
each other; the diagnostic pipeline flags neither as statistically
significant individually, which is the expected behaviour for such a
small sample of extreme events. Plumbing verified.

9. How to use from a notebook or script
---------------------------------------
    from model_risk.adapters.historical_simulation import HistoricalSimulationVaR
    from model_risk.runner import run_backtest
    from model_risk.diagnostics.coverage import (
        kupiec_pof, christoffersen_independence, christoffersen_cc,
        rolling_traffic_light,
    )

    model = HistoricalSimulationVaR(window=250)
    result = run_backtest(
        model=model,
        history=portfolio_pnl_eur.to_frame(),
        positions=None,
        hpl=portfolio_pnl_eur,
        window=('2024-01-02', '2025-12-31'),
        alpha=0.99,
        refit_every='M',
    )

    print(kupiec_pof(result.breach, alpha=0.99))
    print(christoffersen_cc(result.breach, alpha=0.99))
    print(rolling_traffic_light(result.breach).tail())

3. model_risk/adapters/parametric_fx.py -- ParametricFxVaR
----------------------------------------------------------
Implements the VaRModel protocol:

  calibrate(history) expects a DataFrame with MultiIndex columns:
    level 0 in {'stock', 'fx'}, level 1 = ticker or currency code.
    Internally it estimates sigma_ann, cov_ann and per-stock betas and
    stores them on the instance.

  forecast(positions, horizon=1, alpha=0.99) expects positions to be a
    pd.Series keyed by ticker of EUR-denominated values (quantity *
    price * spot already translated upstream). Cash per currency is
    looked up via the cash_by_ccy_fn callable injected at construction:

        ParametricFxVaR(currency_map={'APP': 'USD', ...},
                        cash_by_ccy_fn=lambda date: {'EUR': ..., ...})

    The callable receives the forecast date (or None) so the same
    adapter works with static dicts (ignore the argument) or a per-date
    lookup once a cash-history frame exists.

  Returns VaRForecast(var, es=None, meta={per_currency, nav_eur, z}).
  ES under Normal is a two-line addition (sigma * phi(z) / (1 - alpha))
  but was left out of v1 because the backtest harness does not yet test
  ES; it will go in alongside the Acerbi-Szekely ES test.

Why positions-in-EUR rather than quantities: the alternative --
accepting quantities + a second prices frame at forecast time -- would
duplicate logic already present in pnl.compute_hpl_eur and would force
the runner to carry FX-spot data through a second pathway. Doing the
translation once, upstream, is cleaner. The pivot section (end of file)
describes the helper that now performs that translation from today's
broker-statement snapshot onto every day of the history window.

Two tests run against a 500-day synthetic FX and stock panel:

  (a) Free-function path, compute_fx_var with the same portfolio shape
      as the notebook (APP + LLY in USD, a CHF equity, PNDORA.CO in DKK,

  (b) Adapter + runner walk-forward over a 130-day window at 99% 1-day

      instance calibrates on the MultiIndex history, retrieves cash from
      the lookup function, and emits a VaR per day. The mean VaR is
      stable across the window because the constant-book placeholder
      holds positions fixed, which is exactly what you expect.

Important caveat surfaced by (b): in the synthetic run the breach rate
was far above the 1% target, because the realised HPL contained *total*
P&L (equity + FX) while the FX model only claims to forecast the FX
component. The honest comparison is HPL-FX-component vs parametric FX
VaR. A clean decomposition sits at the input side of PLAT, so this is
another reason to keep PLAT on the roadmap.

1. Why the pivot
----------------
The plan called for a Basel-style walk-forward backtest against the
portfolio we actually held on each day of the window. That requires a
daily position snapshot going back ~250 trading days. No such archive
exists in our data -- broker statements are collected for the monthly
risk report and are not stored as a history.

Rather than stall the model-risk workstream, we switch to a
**constant-book** assumption: take today's broker-statement positions
and today's cash by currency, and ask -- under real historical market
data, how would the parametric FX VaR model have called risk on this
exact book, and how often would the realised hypothetical P&L have
broken through the VaR line.

This is not a Basel-compliant backtest. It tests the *model*, not the
model-on-the-actual-book. Interpreted honestly it catches several real
failure modes -- VaR that never adapts to changing vol, VaR scaled
wrong by orders of magnitude, breach clusters that suggest missing
risk factors -- but it cannot distinguish "the model was wrong" from
"we held a different book back then". Coverage-test numbers are
published with that caveat attached.

    broadcasts them across every day in eur_prices. The `quantities`
    frame is what `compute_hpl_eur` needs to build HPL; the

  - adapters/parametric_fx.py docstring was cleaned up: references to

  - examples/fx_backtest_constant_book.py is a runnable script that

        python -m model_risk.examples.fx_backtest_constant_book

3. First real-data run -- numbers and interpretation
----------------------------------------------------
Using the current book exactly as entered in the FX notebook, the
constant-book backtest from 2024-06-03 to 2025-10-15 (333 trading days)
gives:

    Breaches       : 61  (18.32% vs 1.00% expected)
    VaR (EUR)      : min 40.98   mean 51.26   max 66.42
    HPL (EUR)      : min -336.62  max 302.97

    Kupiec POF           LR = 250.16,  p < 1e-10   -> REJECT
    Christoffersen ind   LR =   1.00,  p = 0.32    -> do not reject
    Christoffersen CC    LR = 251.16,  p < 1e-10   -> REJECT
    Rolling traffic light (end): red, multiplier 4.0

The Kupiec rejection is expected and does not indicate FX-model
failure. The HPL series contains the **total** daily P&L on the book
(equity + FX), while the parametric FX VaR forecasts only the FX
component. Equity volatility on a ~6k EUR book is very much larger
than FX volatility, so every non-trivial equity move breaks through
an FX-only VaR.

The informative reading of this run is the Christoffersen independence
test: p = 0.32, failing to reject iid breaches. That tells us the
FX-VaR level, small as it is, is not producing *clustered* breaches.
The model's day-to-day stability and refit cadence are fine; it is
simply scoped narrower than the P&L stream it is being measured
against.


4. What this unblocks -- and what it flags as urgent
----------------------------------------------------
Unblocked:
  - The full pipeline -- download -> calibrate -> walk-forward ->
    coverage tests -> traffic light -> multiplier -- is now exercised
    end-to-end on real data. Any future model adapter plugs into the
    same script with a three-line swap.
  - The monthly FX risk report can cite a backtest number (with the
    honest caveat) rather than an unvalidated VaR.

Flagged:
  - Decomposing HPL into FX vs equity contributions is now the single
    most useful next piece of work. Without it, no meaningful breach
    count can be reported against the FX model. The decomposition is
    straightforward: for each day, FX contribution = sum_i V_i *
    r_FX_i; equity contribution = HPL - FX contribution. This is a
    small function in pnl.py and does not require any new data.


5. Operational note for the presentation
----------------------------------------
When presenting this work, lead with the *framework* (protocol,
runner, diagnostics, traffic light) as the deliverable. The numbers
above are a plumbing verification on real data, not a model verdict.
The red zone is a property of the comparison, not a property of the
FX model.

1. Why now
----------
`backtesting/backtesting_framework.py` existed as an empty file; the
design was fully written up in backtesting/report/backtesting_report.tex
("A Backtesting Framework for Systematic Strategy Research") but no
code backed it. Every member who wanted to test an idea was
re-implementing their own loop, which is the exact problem the report
sets out to solve.

This pass implements a minimum vertical slice of the design: the
protocol, the engine, a data layer, one concrete strategy, one
YAML config, and the results directory the report promises. It runs
end-to-end today, on real yfinance data. Nothing in it is
risk-department specific; it is a club-wide tool.


2. What shipped
---------------
  - backtesting/strategy.py
        A typing.Protocol `Strategy` with a single method
        `generate_signals(prices) -> target_weights`. Strategies take
        only their own hyper-parameters at __init__; data loading,
        rebalancing, costs and accounting are the engine's job.

  - backtesting/data_store.py
        `DataStore.load_universe(tickers, start, end, freq)` returning a
        rows=dates x cols=tickers close-price frame. Today wraps
        yfinance; swap for Parquet or SQL later by editing this file
        only. Deliberately keeps base currency = listing currency
        (typically USD); the model-risk workstream runs in EUR, the
        strategy backtester does not.

  - backtesting/backtesting_framework.py
        Config-driven engine:
          - load_config(path): YAML loader.
          - _import_strategy(class_name): dynamic import from
            backtesting.strategies.<name>.
          - BacktestEngine.run(strategy): price loading, signal ->
            weight normalisation against max_leverage, rebalance-day
            masking, lagged weights * asset returns, turnover costs,
            equity curve, summary stats.
          - run_from_config(path): full pipeline + results I/O.
          - main()/__main__: CLI entry point.

  - backtesting/strategies/momentum.py
        Cross-sectional momentum: top-quantile long, bottom-quantile
        short. Signals are +/- 1 per asset per day. All row-level
        scaling (to meet max leverage) is done by the engine, not by
        the strategy, so this class stays 15 lines.

  - backtesting/configs/momentum.yml
        Example config: 10 US large-caps, 60-day lookback, top/bottom
        quantile 0.2, monthly rebalance, 3 bps slippage + 1 bp fees,
        max leverage 1.0, 2020-01-01 to 2024-12-31, 1MM initial capital.

  - results/ gitignored; configs in the repo are the source of truth,
    outputs are regenerable.


3. Summary metrics per run
--------------------------
Each run writes to `results/<output.path>/`:
  - config.yml     (copy of the config used)
  - equity.csv
  - returns.csv
  - weights.csv
  - turnover.csv
  - costs.csv
  - meta.json      (timestamp, summary metrics)

`meta.json` / `summary` currently carries:
  total_return, annualised_return, annualised_vol, sharpe,
  max_drawdown, avg_turnover_per_day, n_rebalances,
  n_assets_average_long, n_assets_average_short.

5. Deliberate deviations from backtesting_report.tex
----------------------------------------------------
The report was written as a spec; v1 intentionally differs in the
following places, all documented in-file (class docstring at the top of
backtesting/backtesting_framework.py) and in an appendix added to the
report itself:

  - `rebalance_frequency` lives under `backtest:` instead of
    `strategy.parameters:`. Rebalance cadence is an engine concern, not
    a strategy one; the MomentumStrategy in the report held the field
    but never used it, which was the signal.
  - `data.universe` is a list of tickers, not a named universe like
    `sp500`. Universe registries are a clear v2 extension; v1 takes a
    concrete list so the engine is usable today.
  - `data.source: "local_parquet"` is not implemented. Setting it to
    anything other than `"yfinance"` raises NotImplementedError so the
    promise is explicit.
  - `execution.trade_at_open: true` is accepted but not enforced
    (we only load close prices). `risk.vol_target` likewise accepted
    but not enforced. Listed as planned knobs in the appendix.
